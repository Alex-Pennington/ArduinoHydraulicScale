# PLC #4 — MCP3421 loop ADC, LCD2004A display, discrete buttons

Design, 2026-09-16, branch `feat/plc4-mcp3421-lcd2004` off `plc4/mqtt-port` at `b679d3e`.

**Status: DESIGN ONLY. No firmware edited.** Prior art is
`Alex-Pennington/LogSplitter_Monitor`, which runs all three of these on the **same board**
(UNO R4 WiFi) in production. Read `notes/2026-09-13-4-20ma-loop-decisions.md` and
`MA-LOOP-DESIGN.md` first — this builds directly on both and reuses their reasoning rather than
restating it.

**The machine is benched for this upgrade** (Alex, 2026-09-16), so there is no running scale to
protect and no time pressure. Getting it right beats getting it quick.

---

## 1. What this changes, and the one thing it is NOT for

Three swaps, all of them onto the **Qwiic / Wire1** bus, which PLC #4 does not currently use at
all:

| from | to | why |
|---|---|---|
| internal ADC on A2, referenced to AVCC | **MCP3421**, own 2.048 V reference | removes the supply rail from the measurement |
| 16x2 parallel LCD on D4-D10 | **LCD2004A** 20x4, I2C @ 0x27 | 80 characters instead of 32 |
| resistor-ladder keypad on A0 | **discrete buttons** on freed digital pins | removes the ADC-resolution trap |

**THE MCP3421 IS NOT FOR THE RESOLUTION.** §4 shows the resolution actually drops. It is for the
**reference**, and that is the whole case — see §3.

**Explicitly rejected from the same prior art, so nobody re-proposes them:**

- **INA219** — a DC *power* monitor (16 V bus, 400 mA). It cannot read a 4-20 mA instrument
  loop: at its shunt, 4-20 mA is 0.4-2 mV against a +/-320 mV range. "Current sensor" reads like
  it should fit and it does not. *Possible future use:* watching the 24 V loop supply itself, to
  distinguish "supply died" from "wire cut" — both currently present as `LOOP_DEAD`. Would need
  the 32 V variant. **Not in this design.**
- **TCA9548A I2C multiplexer** — solves address collisions. LCD2004A is 0x27 and MCP3421 is 0x68.
  There is no collision. YAGNI.
- **NAU7802 load cell** — direct force measurement. Out of scope by Alex's ruling, 2026-09-16:
  this design is an upgrade to the **pressure-inference** path, not a replacement for it.

## 2. Burden resistor: 150 ohm becomes 68 ohm

**The merged 150 ohm burden is incompatible with the MCP3421 and would clip below full scale.**
At PGA=1 the MCP3421's input range is +/-2.048 V, so 2.048 / 150 = **13.65 mA** — the reading
would saturate well before the 20 mA span end.

The constraint is `I x R <= 2.048 V`. Largest burden for each current that must stay measurable:

| must stay on scale | largest R |
|---|---|
| 20.0 mA — span end | 102.4 ohm |
| 21.0 mA — NAMUR NE43 fault-high | 97.5 ohm |
| 25.0 mA — conventional hard-over ceiling | 81.9 ohm |
| **29.7 mA — what PLC #3 actually METERED** | **68.9 ohm** |

**68 ohm**, the largest E24 value satisfying the binding constraint. This is the same reasoning
that chose 150 ohm against a 5 V AVCC and rejected 200 ohm: size for the hard-over somebody has
actually **measured** on this estate (playbook §10, PLC #3's tension amplifier at 1.48x its rated
maximum), not for the one the datasheet implies.

**Specify 68 ohm, 0.1%, <=25 ppm/degC.** Burden tolerance remains a direct gain term; that did
not change.

Landmarks at 68 ohm:

| mA | volts | note |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.000 | open loop |
| 3.5 | 0.238 | dead-loop floor |
| 4.0 | 0.272 | live zero |
| 20.0 | 1.360 | span end |
| 21.0 | 1.428 | NE43 fault-high |
| 25.0 | 1.700 | conventional hard-over |
| 29.7 | 2.020 | the measured hard-over, still on scale |
| 30.1 | 2.048 | clip |

**Loop budget is not a constraint.** 1.360 V of burden drop at 20 mA leaves 22.6 V of a 24 V
supply for a transmitter needing roughly 10.5 V.

## 3. Why this is worth doing: it retires issue #4 by construction

`greybeard-plc4-tractor-scale#4` records that **AVCC is now a direct gain term on every pressure
reading and has never been measured.** The retired ratiometric transducer tracked AVCC so the
error cancelled; a burden resistor does not cancel, and the R4's internal ADC references a rail
that on USB power sits anywhere in 4.75-5.25 V. That is up to **+/-5% gain error against a
transmitter rated +/-1.0% FS** — the unmeasured term is potentially five times the sensor's own
specified accuracy.

**The MCP3421 carries its own 2.048 V reference.** The board's supply rail leaves the measurement
entirely. This is the same shape as the burden resistor retiring the floating-input defect: a
hardware change that removes a failure mode rather than a firmware change that detects it.

It is also **differential**, so the burden is sensed across itself rather than against board
ground — better rejection of the common-mode noise a tractor produces.

**Issue #4 should be closed by this change, not by a meter reading.**

## 4. Resolution: 14-bit, NOT 18-bit — and the reason is rate

The MCP3421 trades resolution against sample rate, and they are not independent:

| bits | SPS |
|---|---|
| 18 | 3.75 |
| 16 | 15 |
| 14 | **60** |
| 12 | 240 |

`src/ArduinoHydraulicScale.cpp` samples every **50 ms** (`SAMPLE_SPACING_MS = 50`), averaging
`SAMPLE_COUNT = 10` for a 500 ms reading. That needs **>= 20 SPS**.

- **18-bit at 3.75 SPS cannot keep up.** Ten samples would take **2.7 seconds**, and the operator
  would be watching a scale that lags the load by seconds.
- **16-bit at 15 SPS is also too slow** — 667 ms per sample against a 50 ms budget.
- **14-bit at 60 SPS fits with margin** (16.7 ms per conversion).

**And resolution was never the binding term**, which the 2026-09-01 note established and this
design does not relitigate: at 14-bit the LSB is 250 uV, which across 68 ohm is 3.68 uA, i.e.
**0.689 psi/count — still 43x finer than the sensor's own +/-30 psi.**

So the internal ADC's 14 bits are traded for the MCP3421's 14 bits at the same nominal
resolution, and **every bit of the benefit is in the reference.** Buying the 18-bit headline
would have made the scale sluggish in exchange for precision the sensor cannot deliver.

## 5. Constants: the derivation survives, the numbers move

This is what the mA-as-source-of-truth design was built for. **Only the two hardware facts
change; every threshold re-derives.**

```cpp
constexpr float BURDEN_OHMS      = 68.0f;    // 0.1%, <=25 ppm/degC, across the MCP3421 input
constexpr float MCP3421_VREF_V   = 2.048f;   // the chip's OWN reference - not the board rail
constexpr int   MCP3421_BITS     = 14;       // 60 SPS; see §4, this is a RATE choice
constexpr float MCP3421_LSB_V    = 2.0f * MCP3421_VREF_V / (float)(1 << MCP3421_BITS);  // 250 uV

constexpr int mAToCounts(float mA) {
  return (int)(mA * BURDEN_OHMS / 1000.0f / MCP3421_LSB_V + 0.5f);
}
```

`LOOP_DEAD_MA`, `LOOP_ZERO_MA`, `LOOP_FS_MA` and `LOOP_FAULT_HIGH_MA` are **unchanged** — they are
NAMUR figures and cite standards, not hardware. The derived counts become:

| constant | mA | old (150 ohm, AVCC) | **new (68 ohm, 2.048 V, 14-bit)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `COUNTS_DEAD` | 3.5 | 1720 | **952** |
| `COUNTS_ZERO` | 4.0 | 1966 | **1088** |
| `COUNTS_FS` | 20.0 | 9830 | **5440** |
| `COUNTS_FAULT_HIGH` | 21.0 | 10321 | **5712** |

All four land exactly on integers — `mA x 68 x 4` — with no rounding to argue about.

`classifyLoop`, `loopIsMeasuring`, `loopStateName`, `countsToPSI` and `countsToMilliamps` are
**unchanged in logic**; they consume the derived constants. `test/test_loop_scaling.cpp`'s four
`static_assert`s and its band-edge checks move to the new values and remain the gate.

**`ADC_VREF_V = 5.0f` and `ADC_FS = 16383` are deleted.** They described the internal ADC. Note
`vReference` was already collapsed into `ADC_VREF_V` in commit `4618826` precisely so one edit
would move everything — that pays off here.

## 6. MCP3421 driver

Port from `LogSplitter_Monitor`'s `include/mcp3421_sensor.h` / `src/mcp3421_sensor.cpp`, which
run this chip on **Wire1** on this exact board. Do not invent one.

- I2C address **0x68**, `Wire1` (Qwiic connector).
- Configure: 14-bit, PGA 1x, **continuous** conversion.
- The read must **detect a not-ready conversion and a bus failure**, and neither may be reported
  as a measurement. A failed I2C read is NOT `0 counts` — 0 counts is `LOOP_DEAD`, an
  affirmative statement that the loop is open. **Conflating them would reintroduce the
  plausible-wrong-answer failure this machine's whole history is about.** A failed read holds the
  previous sample and is counted.

**New state:** an I2C read-failure counter, published in status alongside `overrange` /
`underrange`, so a degrading bus is visible before it is total.

## 7. Display: LCD2004A, and the architecture fix that goes with it

Port the driver from `LogSplitter_Monitor`'s `include/lcd_display.h`, which drives this display
over **Wire1 with direct I2C rather than the LiquidCrystal_I2C library**, deliberately, "for full
control". Address **0x27**.

### The fault row is a property of the DISPLAY LAYER, not of a screen

This is the architectural defect behind the Critical found in the 4-20 mA review: fault
indication lived in `hmiDrawHome()`, and `hmiPoll()` returned early for `SCR_MENU` and
`SCR_CHECKCAL`, so **every screen added later silently opted out of it.** A dead transmitter was
invisible on the Check-cal screen, and freezing the filter had made that stale reading
rock-steady rather than visibly decaying — more convincing, not less.

**Row 3 is reserved and is written on every render, by the display layer, whatever screen is
active.** It shows `sensorStateName()` when `!loopIsMeasuring(loopState)`, the calibration
warning when `calibrationDefaulted`, and is blank when neither. A new screen cannot opt out of it
because no screen writes it.

Rows 0-2 are the screen's own. Indicative home layout, 20 columns, `char[21]` buffers:

```
0:  W  -12345 lb   n 12
1:  psi  2850  mA 19.21
2:  Q3 sending   CAL ok
3:  <fault banner, or blank>
```

`SATURATED` and `UNDER ZERO` still do **not** suppress the weight — they are measuring states, and
withholding a weight because the transmitter is pinned was the old defect, not a safeguard.

## 8. Buttons: `keyIndex()` is the whole seam

The keypad is a resistor ladder **on the 16x2 shield**, so it physically leaves with the display.
Five discrete buttons on freed digital pins replace it, with `INPUT_PULLUP`.

**The change is one function.** `keyIndex()` returns `0..4` (Right, Up, Down, Left, Select) or
`-1`, and everything downstream consumes only that integer — the debounce-on-change, the stable-key
latch, and `hmiOnPress` firing on **press only, never release or hold**. That logic is proven and
is the part worth keeping; only its source changes.

**THE ONE REAL HAZARD, and a careless implementation will miss it.** A ladder is a voltage divider
and can report exactly one key. Discrete buttons can be pressed **simultaneously**, and the
downstream logic assumes "one key or none" — two at once would present as chatter between two
stable values. **`keyIndex()` scans in fixed priority order and returns the first pressed**,
preserving the existing contract exactly.

**Removed with the ladder:** every `analogReadResolution(10)` / `(14)` flip. That trap is
documented in three places in this firmware and disappears entirely once nothing shares an ADC
between a keypad and a sensor. `keyName()` and the `keys` console diagnostic lose their reason to
exist and go with it.

## 9. What this removes

- `LiquidCrystal` parallel driver and pins `pin_RS`, `pin_EN`, `pin_d4..d7`, `pin_BL` — **seven
  digital pins freed**, five of which the buttons take.
- `analogReadResolution` calls — all of them.
- `keyName()`, `keyIndex()`'s ladder thresholds, and the `keys` console command.
- `ADC_VREF_V`, `ADC_FS`.
- `analogInPin` / A2 use for the sensor. **A2 becomes free.**

---

## 10. What this does NOT change

- **The MQTT wire contract.** No field added, removed or renamed, except the new I2C-failure
  counter in status. `greybeard-plc-consumer` PR #11 and migration 061 are unaffected and
  nothing there needs re-doing.
- The five loop states, both predicates, and every rule about which states measure.
- `psi` publishing JSON null, `psi_fault` direction, the filter freeze on fault, the queue, the
  tally, the EEPROM signature and calibration flow.
- **The weight path.** `outputValue = calFactor * (smoothedValue - tareValue)` is untouched.
  Counts change scale, so **a stored calibration is invalidated exactly as it was by the 4-20 mA
  swap** — `EEPROM_SIGNATURE` must bump again, to `PL4B`, so a `PL4A` calibration cannot restore
  and read wrong. Expect `CAL NOT SET!` and a full tare and `kv` after flashing.

## 11. Residuals — carried forward or newly created

- **The 3.5-4.0 mA dead band** still reads as a healthy 0 psi. Unchanged.
- **`psi_fault` does not latch.** Unchanged.
- **Burden tolerance** remains a direct gain term; 0.1% at 68 ohm.
- **NEW: the I2C bus becomes a single point of failure for the measurement AND the display.**
  Under the internal ADC they were independent. A Qwiic connector working loose now takes both.
  This is the cost of the design and it is why §6 requires a bus-failure path that is
  distinguishable from a dead loop.
- **NEW: the LCD2004A backpack is typically a 5 V part on a 3.3 V Qwiic bus.** I2C is open-drain
  so this generally works and `LogSplitter_Monitor` runs exactly this combination — but it is
  proven on a sibling, **not measured here.** Check the levels before assuming.
- **Nothing here is flashed or bench-tested.** Every claim is read from source, from the sibling
  repo, or derived; none is observed on hardware.
